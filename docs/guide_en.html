KB Bridge Build Guide

日本語

KB Bridge is an adapter that turns a wired QMK / Vial keyboard into a Bluetooth LE keyboard. This guide covers soldering the HY0020 (an nRF52832 BLE module), writing the firmware and assembling the case.

2. Write the CH559

Before soldering the HY0020, write the firmware into the USB chip (CH559) with the CH559 first flash page, in Chrome or Edge on a computer.

  1. Keep the two JP1 holes (silkscreen "BOOT") shorted, for example with the end of a jumper wire, while you plug J1 (the USB-C receptacle) into the computer.
  2. Press "Write" on the page, choose the WCH device (4348:55E0) in the list and press "Connect".
  3. When it says "Done", unplug the USB cable and remove the JP1 short.

On Windows, install the WinUSB driver with Zadig once first (the CH559 first flash page explains how). Without the HY0020 the LED stays dark after writing. To check it, plug it in while holding PAIR: a "KB Bridge Update" serial port should appear on the computer.

3. Solder the HY0020

Orientation: the AE-HY0020-DIP sits directly on the bottom side of the board (the side with fewer parts), with no gap. Point the HY0020 module (the part with the metal lid) towards J1 (the USB-C receptacle, silkscreen "BATTERY / PC") and line it up with the outline on the bottom silkscreen.

  1. Push the thin pin header through both the KB Bridge board and the AE-HY0020-DIP from the component side. The plastic part stays on the component side.
  2. Hold things in place with masking tape on the side opposite the one you solder: the AE-HY0020-DIP on the bottom side, the header's plastic on the component side.
  3. Solder the bottom side (the AE-HY0020-DIP side). Cut the protruding pins with nippers and tidy up the joints.
  4. On the component side, cut the pins together with the plastic. Leave 2 mm or less sticking out so they do not touch the case.
  5. Solder the component side too. Both sides need soldering.

In the case, the module side (the bottom side) faces away from everything else, which keeps the BLE signal strong.

4. Write the firmware

Use the firmware updater in Chrome or Edge on a computer.

  1. Hold the PAIR button and plug J1 (the USB-C receptacle) into the computer. You can let go once it is plugged in.
  2. Press "Write" on the page, choose "KB Bridge Update" in the list and press "Connect".
  3. When it says "Done", unplug the USB cable and plug it in again without pressing PAIR.

On the first start the LED may alternate red and green for a few seconds: the CH559 firmware is being updated as well. Just wait. Later updates use the same steps.

5. Check it works

  1. Plug J1 into a USB power source (a computer or a power bank) without pressing PAIR. A short green blink every second means it is waiting for a connection.
  2. Pair with "KB Bridge" in the Bluetooth settings of your computer or phone. Once connected, the LED stays green for 2 seconds.
  3. Plug the keyboard into J2 (the USB-C plug). The keyboard works after about 2 seconds.

6. Assemble the case

  1. Place the board in the bottom case, with J1 and J2 lined up with their openings.
  2. Put the top case on and fasten it with the four included M2 × 8 mm screws. Do not overtighten.

In use, the flat face (the bottom of the bottom case) faces up. PAIR and LOW-I can be pressed through the case from the top.

7. Using it

Buttons

PAIR and LOW-I can be pressed through the case from the top. Their positions are shown below.

ButtonActionResult
PAIRShort pressStart waiting for a connection (to pair with another device)
PAIRHold 3 sForget all pairings (LED red ×3)
PAIRHold while plugging in USBFirmware update mode
LOW-IPressCycle the power-bank keep-alive: weak → strong → off (saved; default weak)

Power-bank keep-alive (LOW-I)

KB Bridge draws so little current when idle that a power bank may decide it is not in use and switch itself off. The keep-alive draws a short burst of current now and then to prevent that.

SettingLEDWhat it does
Weak (default)Green ×250 ms every 10 s, ~0.75 mA on average. Try this first
StrongGreen ×3200 ms every 5 s, ~6 mA on average. For power banks that still switch off with weak
OffRed ×2On a computer or a supply without auto-off

LED

PatternMeaning
Green every secondWaiting for a connection
Green for 2 sConnected
Red every 2 sConnected, but no keyboard
Red and green alternatingUpdating the USB chip firmware
Fast red blinkingUSB chip fault (write the firmware again)
One red blink every 5 sSupply voltage low (below 4.5 V)

Current draw (for reference)

KB Bridge also powers the keyboard, so battery life depends mostly on the keyboard. Ordinary wired keyboards are not designed for low power the way wireless keyboards are, so the battery runs down faster than with a typical wireless keyboard. Examples measured with a USB current meter at 5 V:

StateCurrent
KB Bridge alone (no keyboard, keep-alive off)The meter shows 0.00 A (below 1 mA)
Typing on an attached keyboardAbout 11 mA (with the keyboard we measured)
Keep-alive "weak"Adds about 0.75 mA on average
Keep-alive "strong"Adds about 6 mA on average

Keyboards with RGB lighting draw much more. Without input the keyboard is put into USB suspend after 2 minutes; a keyboard that saves power in suspend (QMK's rgb_matrix.sleep and the like) then draws much less.

Automatic power-off

After 10 minutes without a connection to any device, KB Bridge powers off (the LED goes dark) and so does the attached keyboard. Press LOW-I or unplug and replug the keyboard to start it again; it goes back to waiting for a connection (the LOW-I press that wakes it does not change the keep-alive setting). PAIR does not wake it.

On a power bank, the keep-alive stops while KB Bridge is off, so the power bank may switch off too. Switch the power bank back on in that case.

Vial

With a Vial-enabled keyboard you can change the keymap in Vial while connected over Bluetooth. Key remapping is done on the keyboard (in Vial), not in KB Bridge.

Vial responds slowly over Bluetooth, though, so for configuring it is better to connect the keyboard to the computer directly with a USB cable.

8. Troubleshooting

ProblemWhat to do
The LED never lightsCheck the HY0020 soldering, then write the firmware again with the updater
"KB Bridge" does not show up in the Bluetooth listShort-press PAIR to start waiting for a connection. If a device you paired before still has KB Bridge registered, remove it in that device's Bluetooth settings
It will not connect, or keeps disconnectingHold PAIR for 3 s to forget the pairings, remove KB Bridge on the other device as well, and pair again
The power bank switches offPress LOW-I to choose "strong" (green ×3)
On Linux (Ubuntu etc.) typing stops working after a while; reconnecting Bluetooth fixes itThe computer's Bluetooth adapter power saving (USB autosuspend) causes this. If cat /sys/module/btusb/parameters/enable_autosuspend prints Y, run echo 'options btusb enable_autosuspend=0' | sudo tee /etc/modprobe.d/btusb.conf and restart the computer (it should then print N)
Writing stops part way or failsUnplug, plug in again while holding PAIR and write again. An interrupted write is fixed by writing again. If it keeps failing, clone the GitHub repository and write from your own computer (see "Local testing" in the README)
"KB Bridge Update" does not appear on the updaterThe CH559 may not have been written. Redo 2. Write the CH559 (this also works with the HY0020 fitted). If it still does not appear, check that you hold PAIR while plugging in and that the cable carries data

9. Make your own case

You can also design your own case instead of using the included one. A 3D model and a dimension drawing of the board are provided.

  • kb_bridge_v0.7_pcba.stl (the board with the HY0020 fitted, in mm. Origin at the board centre, +Y towards J1, Z = 0 at the bottom face of the board)
  • kb_bridge_v0.7_dimensions.pdf (outline, mounting holes, connectors, buttons, LED, heights)

Power through the 5V / GND pins

The two holes near J1 (silkscreen "+5V" and "GND", 2.54 mm pitch) can also supply power, for example from a battery or a power board inside your case.

  • About 4.5–5.5 V as a guide. They connect to the same input as J1, which is protected against reverse polarity.
  • Powering through these pins is not guaranteed to work. Check it with your actual supply before relying on it.
  • Do not connect different power sources to J1 and these pins at the same time.

Notes for your case

  • Leave a hole where the LED (component side, (−14.0, 8.0) from the centre) can be seen.
  • Keep metal away from the HY0020 module (bottom side, J1 end); it weakens the Bluetooth signal.
  • Make sure PAIR and LOW-I can be pressed.

10. Source code

The source code of the firmware and the flashing pages is on GitHub (darakuneko/kb_bridge) under the MIT license. If something bothers you or you want KB Bridge to do something else, change it as you like. If you build a good feature, pull requests are welcome.
